1 · Elite YC Strategy Layer

Synthesis of what actually moves applications from "skip" to "interview" — based on patterns from accepted batches, partner essays, and post-mortems.

The single mental model: A YC partner reads ~50 applications in a sitting. They spend 60–90 seconds on yours before deciding "interview / maybe / no." Every sentence has to earn its place. Vague is a no. Polished is a no. Specific, honest, and shipped is a yes.

What accepted founders did right

They launched before they applied

Almost every YES has the phrase "we launched on [date]" with real users by application time. Not a beta waitlist. Real users doing the thing.

They wrote like a text message

Short sentences. No corporate verbs. "We help X do Y" not "We're a platform empowering X to optimize Y." If your mom can't understand the first sentence, rewrite it.

They had insider insight

One non-obvious thing they know about the market because they lived it. For Alma: "Cold applications convert at <1%. Warm intros convert at 30%+. No one teaches students how to run that funnel."

They showed velocity

"We built this in 4 weekends." "We shipped v1 in 11 days." YC funds rate of progress more than current state. Make velocity legible.

They were brutally honest about traction

"47 signups, 12 active weekly, 1 paying" beats "strong early interest." Partners can smell exaggeration in two seconds and it kills trust.

They had a wedge, not a vision

Don't sell the 10-year empire. Sell the painfully specific first user. "Brown & Rice undergrads applying to summer internships" is a wedge. "Career platform" is not.

Why applications fail (avoid all of these)

  • Solution-in-search-of-a-problem framing. Starting with the AI/tech and not the human pain. YC funds problems first.
  • "We will" instead of "we have." Future tense in 2026 is a death sentence. Replace every "will" with "did" or cut it.
  • Bloated TAM math. "$300B career services market" reads as lazy. Pick a precise beachhead and own it.
  • Two co-founders who sound interchangeable. If both bios say "passionate about X," partners assume you'll fight in 6 months. Differentiate sharply.
  • No demo, or a 4-minute demo. If they have to dig to see the product, they won't. Show it in the first 30 seconds of the video.
  • Hedging. "We think we might be able to..." Pick a side. Be wrong with conviction.
  • Polished video with music + b-roll. Reads as "they spent a week on this instead of building." Unscripted founders > production.
  • Submitting at the deadline. Last-hour applications signal poor planning. Submit ~24h early.

What YC partners explicitly look for

Clarity of thought

Can you compress your idea into one sentence a 12-year-old understands?

Founder–market fit

Why is this the most obvious thing for you to be working on, and not someone else?

Default-alive math

Even hand-wavy: how does this make money, and roughly how much per user?

Schlep tolerance

Are you willing to do the boring, manual work (e.g., calling 100 students one by one)?

Speed of execution

How fast did you ship v1? How many cycles per week are you running?

Real conviction

Will you still be working on this in 5 years if it's hard? Why?

2 · 7-Day Execution Plan

Each day has one job. If a task doesn't directly improve the app, the pilot, or the application — it's deferred until after submission. Founder A = Frontend/Product. Founder B = Backend/Integrations.

Sprint rule: Cut anything that isn't on the path to "20 real Brown/Rice students used Alma this week." That metric, in your application, is worth more than any feature.
Day 01 · Friday
Lock the Story. Cut the Scope.
Strategy Day
🎯 Goal: End the day with a one-sentence pitch, a brutally cut MVP scope, and a list of 30 named pilot users you'll text on Monday.
🛠 Founder A (Frontend / Product)
  • Write 5 versions of the one-sentence pitch. Pick the best with co-founder.
  • Audit the app: list every screen, mark each ship, trim, or defer.
  • Define the single golden flow: Resume upload → company → outreach draft → sent. Everything else is optional for v1.
  • Draft pilot user list: 30 names from your Brown/Rice networks (clubs, dorms, classes).
🛠 Founder B (Backend / Integrations)
  • List every connector/integration still needed. Rank by impact on the golden flow.
  • Set hard scope: only ship what the golden flow requires. Nothing else.
  • Set up basic event tracking (PostHog or simple Supabase logs): signups, outreach sent, replies marked.
  • Confirm Claude API quota and Serper API quota for ~50 users worth of usage.

✅ End-of-Day Checklist

  • One-sentence pitch written, agreed, and pinned in shared doc
  • MVP scope list — every screen labeled ship/trim/defer
  • Golden flow documented as 5 numbered steps
  • Pilot list of 30 named students with contact method
  • Event tracking sending events to a dashboard you can both see
  • Both founders agree on what is not shipping this week
⚠ Avoid: Adding features today. Adding features. Discussing the logo. Discussing the name. Building anything. Today is decisions only.
Day 02 · Saturday
Build the Spine. End-to-End.
Build Day
🎯 Goal: By midnight, both founders can log into a fresh account, upload a real resume, and get a real outreach draft for a real alumnus. Even if ugly.
🛠 Founder A (Frontend / Product)
  • Polish the onboarding: resume upload → profile review. Cut copy by 50%.
  • Tighten the company select screen — empty states, loading states, error states.
  • Outreach draft view: clear "Send via Email" and "Send via LinkedIn" CTAs.
  • Strip every "coming soon" badge. If it's not done, hide it.
🛠 Founder B (Backend / Integrations)
  • Ship LinkedIn enrichment connector end-to-end (Serper integration confirmed working).
  • Wire email send (Resend or SendGrid) for draft → actually delivered.
  • Lock auth flow (Supabase) — signup, login, session persistence on production.
  • Push to a stable preview URL (not localhost). Send link to Founder A.

✅ End-of-Day Checklist

  • Stable production URL exists and works on mobile + desktop
  • Co-founder created a fresh account and completed the golden flow without a bug
  • One real outreach was sent through the system to a real alumnus
  • All non-shipping screens are hidden or feature-flagged off
  • Bug list created and triaged: blockers vs polish
⚠ Avoid: Refactoring. Renaming variables. "While I'm in here" changes. Anything that touches code outside the golden flow.
Day 03 · Sunday
Bug Bash + Dogfood Hard.
Polish Day
🎯 Goal: Both founders run through the entire app three times each as different personas. Fix every blocker. Ship 5 friends from your network as Day-0 pilots tonight.
🛠 Founder A (Frontend / Product)
  • Run 3 personas through the app: CS junior, Econ senior, undecided sophomore.
  • Fix every visual bug that breaks trust (overflow, broken icons, jank).
  • Write the SMS/iMessage you'll send to pilots Monday morning. ~2 sentences.
  • Recruit 5 friends to test tonight. Watch them use it without explaining anything.
🛠 Founder B (Backend / Integrations)
  • Burn down blocker list from Day 2. No new features.
  • Set rate limits, basic abuse protection, monitoring/alerts on errors.
  • Verify event tracking captures: signup, resume_uploaded, outreach_drafted, outreach_sent, reply_marked.
  • Build a simple internal dashboard showing live counts of those events.

✅ End-of-Day Checklist

  • 5 real friends used the app tonight; you watched at least 2 of them in person/Zoom
  • Top 3 friction points identified — fixed or accepted with eyes open
  • Internal metrics dashboard live and showing today's events
  • Pilot SMS drafted and ready to send Monday 9am
  • Both founders feel "I'd actually use this if I were them" — honestly
⚠ Avoid: Watching friends use the app and explaining what they're "supposed" to do. Sit on your hands. Their confusion is your data.
Day 04 · Monday
Launch the Pilot. Get Real Numbers.
Launch Day
🎯 Goal: 30 pilot invites sent before noon. Aim for 15+ signups, 5+ outreach messages sent through Alma, 1+ reply by end of day. Document every datapoint.
🛠 Founder A (Frontend / Product)
  • Send all 30 personalized SMS/iMessages by 11am. Personal, not blast.
  • Be on-call all day for support — DMs, fixes, copy tweaks.
  • Capture 5 quotes from pilots about the experience (good and bad).
  • Identify 3 power users to interview Tuesday for testimonials.
🛠 Founder B (Backend / Integrations)
  • Watch metrics dashboard live. Hot-fix any production issues within 10 min.
  • Pull a clean stats snapshot at midnight: signups, activations, outreach sent, replies.
  • Investigate any drop-off in the funnel — fix or note for application honesty.
  • Prep a one-page "by the numbers" doc to use in the application Tuesday.

✅ End-of-Day Checklist

  • 30 invites sent (1:1, personal — not a blast)
  • Real numbers captured: signups, activations, outreach sent, replies
  • 5 verbatim quotes from pilots saved to a doc
  • Zero production downtime; any errors logged and patched same day
  • Founders have a shared "today we learned" note (3 bullets, max)
⚠ Avoid: Mass-emailing. Posting on Twitter/LinkedIn for "buzz." This is qualitative pilot data, not a marketing launch. 20 deep users > 200 shallow ones.
Day 05 · Tuesday
Draft the Application. Honest Numbers.
Writing Day
🎯 Goal: Full application drafted by 8pm. Sent to 3 trusted reviewers (1 founder ahead of you, 1 sharp non-founder friend, 1 advisor) for ruthless feedback by midnight.
🛠 Founder A (Frontend / Product)
  • Draft answers to all YC questions using the framework in §3 below.
  • Pull 2–3 best pilot quotes verbatim (with name + school) into "traction" answer.
  • Record a 2-min Loom demo of the actual product in use — clean run-through.
  • Write the founder bios so they sound like 2 different people, not interchangeable twins.
🛠 Founder B (Backend / Integrations)
  • Compile final traction stats from Day 4–5 (DAU, outreach sent, replies, coffees booked).
  • Address Day 4 bugs and polish any rough edges visible in the demo.
  • Push a clean, deployed version of the app for the application URL field.
  • Write the technical "how it works" sentence in plain English (no jargon).

✅ End-of-Day Checklist

  • Every YC question answered in draft, no "TBD" anywhere
  • Traction answer has real numbers and at least one user quote
  • Demo Loom recorded and linked in the application
  • 3 reviewers have the draft and a 36-hour deadline
  • Read the entire application out loud — it sounds like you, not ChatGPT
⚠ Avoid: Letting AI rewrite the answers in "professional" voice. YC partners read AI-flavored answers daily and skip them. Keep your specific phrasing, even if imperfect.
Day 06 · Wednesday
Record the Video. Revise Everything.
Video + Revision Day
🎯 Goal: Founder video shot in 5–10 takes, picked, uploaded. Application revised based on all 3 reviewer rounds. Both founders sleep before midnight.
🛠 Founder A (Frontend / Product)
  • Set up a clean recording spot: even light, plain background, phone on tripod.
  • Run the 60-second beat structure with co-founder. Don't memorize — internalize.
  • Record 8–10 takes. Pick the one that sounds most natural, not the one that's most perfect.
  • Incorporate reviewer feedback into the application. Cut 20% of your word count.
🛠 Founder B (Backend / Integrations)
  • Be on camera for the video — both founders must appear and speak.
  • Speak to the technical/integration insight in your section.
  • Final pass on the deployed app: no broken links, no typos, no console errors.
  • Re-pull updated metrics — application traction stats should be from latest data.

✅ End-of-Day Checklist

  • 60-second video recorded, both founders on camera, single take
  • Video uploaded as unlisted YouTube link (test the link incognito)
  • Application revised, word count down, every sentence earns its spot
  • One founder reads it cold and could explain Alma to a stranger from it
  • Sleep. You ship tomorrow.
⚠ Avoid: Editing the video in iMovie. Adding music. Adding captions. Adding b-roll. The unpolished version is the strong version.
Day 07 · Thursday
Final Pass. Submit Early.
Submit Day
🎯 Goal: Application submitted by noon. Not midnight. Not 11:59. Noon. Then go for a walk.
🛠 Founder A (Frontend / Product)
  • Read the entire application out loud, top to bottom. Fix any awkward sentence.
  • Triple-check every link: app URL, demo Loom, founder LinkedIn, video.
  • Verify video plays in incognito on phone + desktop.
  • Click submit at 11:55am. Screenshot the confirmation.
🛠 Founder B (Backend / Integrations)
  • Verify production app is up, fast, and doesn't error for a brand-new user.
  • Set up an alert if the site goes down between now and YC interview decisions.
  • Pull a final metrics snapshot — keep dated, you'll need it for follow-ups.
  • Sit next to Founder A during submit. Two-person check.

✅ End-of-Day Checklist

  • Application submitted before noon Pacific
  • Confirmation screenshot saved
  • App is live, healthy, and reachable on a fresh device
  • Pilot users have been thanked individually (not in bulk)
  • Founders agree: the application is honest and we're proud of the version we sent
⚠ Avoid: "One more tweak" energy after 11am. Past the deadline you set, the marginal edit is negative EV — small fixes introduce typos under stress.

3 · YC Application Optimization

How to answer the questions that actually decide your application. Strong vs weak phrasing, with examples calibrated to Alma.

Universal rule: Specific is strong. Concrete is strong. Honest is strong. Anything that could appear in any other application is weak. If your answer would still make sense for a fintech startup, rewrite it.
"What are you building?" (the most important question)

What partners are scanning for: A clear, concrete sentence. Who you serve. What you do for them. How. Nothing else in the first sentence.

Weak
"Alma is an AI-powered career platform that empowers Gen-Z students to leverage their networks and unlock opportunities through intelligent matching and personalized outreach."
Strong
"Alma helps Brown and Rice undergrads land internships through warm intros instead of cold applications. We rank companies by which alumni you can reach, then draft the email."
"Why this idea? Why now?"

What partners are scanning for: A specific story that proves the pain is real and that you've personally felt it. Bonus if the story is mildly embarrassing — embarrassment signals authenticity.

Weak
"We believe job searching is broken and AI now makes it possible to fix it. The market is ready for innovation in career services."
Strong
"Last summer I sent 87 cold applications and got 0 interviews. A single warm intro from a Rice alum got me the offer. Every classmate I talk to has the same story — and there's no tool that runs that funnel for you. AI finally makes the alumni-matching + drafting cheap enough to do at scale."
"What traction do you have?"

What partners are scanning for: Real numbers, recently. Even tiny numbers. A small, honest number with a date beats a big vague claim every time.

Weak
"We're seeing strong early traction with significant interest from our target market and a growing waitlist of users."
Strong
"Launched April 20, 2026. In the first 3 days: 47 signups (Brown 28 / Rice 19), 31 completed onboarding, 312 outreach messages sent through Alma, 89 replies, 12 coffee chats booked, 1 internship offer (Maya, Rice junior, Goldman). Daily active sessions trending up week-over-week."
"Why are you the right team?"

What partners are scanning for: Two founders who do different things and who have a non-obvious right to win. Avoid "passionate." Use evidence.

Weak
"We are two passionate technical co-founders with backgrounds in computer science and product. We've worked together on multiple projects and complement each other well."
Strong
"[A] runs Brown CSA's career mentorship program — has personally helped 18 classmates land internships through warm intros over 2 years. [B] built and maintains Rice's Owl Investments alumni database (1,400+ contacts) and shipped 3 production Next.js apps before Alma. We've been roommates' friends since high school and have shipped 4 hackathon projects together."
"What's something most people don't know about your space?"

What partners are scanning for: A non-consensus insight that signals you've actually been in the trenches. This question separates real founders from idea-tourists.

Weak
"Most people don't realize how broken the recruiting process is for students."
Strong
"Alumni are 50× more likely to respond to a student than to a recruiter, but the response rate collapses if the message is generic. The wedge isn't 'find an alum' — it's writing a 4-sentence message that references something specific they actually care about. That's the part everyone gets wrong, including LinkedIn."
"How will you make money?"

What partners are scanning for: A simple, plausible answer. You don't need to be right — you need to have thought about it.

Weak
"We will explore multiple monetization options including B2B partnerships and freemium models."
Strong
"$15/month student subscription after a 30-day free trial. Long-term, universities pay us per active student ($40–60/student/year) — career services budgets average $250/student and the ROI conversation is easy. We'll test the consumer price point in May with the first paying cohort."
"What do you understand about your business that other companies don't?"

What partners are scanning for: Schlep insight. Something hard or unsexy that you're willing to do because you know it matters.

Weak
"We deeply understand our user base and can build the right product for them."
Strong
"The reason this hasn't worked before is that scraping LinkedIn for alumni is brittle and expensive. We get around it by ingesting each university's directory + clubs + sports rosters once, then enriching with public web search per user — cheaper per query, higher quality, and defensible per school. We'll do that boring work school-by-school for the first 30 universities."

4 · Founder Video Framework

60 seconds. Both founders. Single take. No edits. The video is a vibe check, not a pitch — partners are testing if you can communicate clearly under mild pressure.

The bar: If the partner watches all 60 seconds and could repeat your one-sentence pitch back to a colleague, you passed. Everything in the video should serve that outcome.

The 60-second beat structure

0:00 – 0:08
Names + school + one-sentence pitch.
"Hi, I'm [A] from Brown, this is [B] from Rice. We're building Alma — we help college students land internships through warm intros instead of cold applications."
0:08 – 0:22
The personal problem.
One specific story. "I sent 87 applications. Got 0 callbacks. One alum intro got me the offer. Every classmate has the same story." 14 seconds, no more.
0:22 – 0:38
What we built — show the screen if possible.
"You upload your resume, Alma ranks 30 companies by which alumni you can reach, drafts the message, and tracks the funnel. Here's the real one I sent yesterday." Screen-share for 5 seconds.
0:38 – 0:50
Real traction in real numbers.
"We launched four days ago. 47 students from Brown and Rice. 312 messages sent through Alma. 89 replies. One offer." Specific, dated, honest.
0:50 – 1:00
Why us — short and earnest.
"We've been the students struggling with this. We've already shipped four products together. We'll keep building this whether or not we get in." End on a real smile.

What kills videos instantly

  • Reading from a script — partners can hear your eyes moving
  • Music or sound effects of any kind
  • Title cards, lower thirds, slides, motion graphics
  • Multiple cuts edited together (looks rehearsed and slow)
  • Only one founder speaking, the other off-camera
  • Vague claims ("we're seeing tons of interest")
  • Bad audio — phone mic in a noisy room is a no
  • Going over 60 seconds. Strict.

What partners notice and like

  • Both founders on camera, looking into the lens
  • Single take, conversational pace, real energy
  • One specific number ("87 applications, 0 callbacks")
  • 5 seconds of actual product on screen
  • You finishing each other's sentences naturally
  • Co-founder chemistry — visible respect
  • You being recognizably yourselves, not "founders"
  • Submitting under 60 seconds. 55 seconds is great.

Production setup (15 minutes, $0)

ElementSetup
CameraPhone on a tripod or stack of books, eye level
AudioQuiet room, phone within 3 feet of speakers, no echo
LightingWindow in front of you, not behind. Daylight only.
BackgroundPlain wall or messy desk — both fine. Avoid empty white voids.
WardrobeWhat you'd wear to a friend's apartment. Not a blazer.
Take countAim for 8–10 takes. Pick the most natural one, not the most polished.
UploadUnlisted YouTube link. Test in incognito on phone + desktop before submitting.

5 · Standards & Sprint Discipline

The non-negotiables for the next 7 days.

The hierarchy of work

  1. Anything that helps real students use Alma this week
  2. Anything that strengthens application traction numbers
  3. Anything that improves the video or written application
  4. Everything else (defer until after submission)

Daily rituals

  • 9:00 AM — 15-min standup: yesterday's wins, today's one priority, blockers
  • 1:00 PM — silent work block (3 hours, no Slack, no calls)
  • 9:00 PM — 10-min "what we learned" note in shared doc
  • 11:00 PM — laptops closed. Sleep is leverage.

Hard "no" list

  • No new features after Day 2
  • No design system overhauls
  • No domain or branding changes
  • No "let's add one more company" scope creep
  • No mass-marketing posts before submission

Reviewers to recruit (Day 1)

  • One founder who's been through YC (any batch)
  • One sharp non-founder friend who can spot bullshit
  • One advisor or professor with skin in the game
  • Brief them: "Tell me what's confusing or boring, not what you'd add."
You will not get 7 perfect days. You will lose half a day to a bug, a half-day to a fight, a half-day to a friend visiting. Plan for it. The plan above survives losing 1.5 days. It does not survive losing 3. Protect the floor, not the ceiling.
