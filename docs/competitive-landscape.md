# Competitive landscape

For YC application + positioning. None of the players below do what Alma does — operate end-to-end agentically inside the IB recruiting funnel for one specific student demographic. Below: who's adjacent, why we're different, what we'd say to a YC partner asking "but isn't this just X?"

## Direct competitors (alumni networking + AI)

### Wellfound (formerly AngelList Talent)
- Broad job board with messaging.
- **Different from Alma:** generalist startups, not finance. No agent — it's a marketplace. No outreach drafting. Brown/Rice students don't break into banking through Wellfound.

### LunchClub
- Random alumni intros via AI matchmaking.
- **Different from Alma:** intro-only — no follow-through. Doesn't draft, doesn't track replies, doesn't advance pipeline. One-shot, not pipeline.

### Handshake
- University job board (every Brown/Rice student already uses it).
- **Different from Alma:** application portal. Doesn't help with the part that actually matters (networking calls). Bankers ignore Handshake messages.

## Adjacent (cold outreach AI for sales)

### Smartwriter / Lavender / Apollo Sequences
- AI-drafted outbound for sales teams.
- **Different from Alma:** built for SDRs at scale, not students breaking into one industry. No alumni-warmth scoring, no IB-specific voice, no 7-stage pipeline matching the actual recruiting funnel. Generic enough that bankers spot the AI tells immediately.

### Lemlist
- Outbound automation + cold email sequences.
- **Different from Alma:** template-driven. Not personalized per banker. Not student-voiced. Same critique as Lavender.

## IB-specific (closest in spirit, different mechanics)

### Forage
- Free virtual experience programs (banks pay Forage).
- **Different from Alma:** training, not connections. Resume-line generator. Doesn't get the analyst on a call.

### Wall Street Mastermind
- Premium IB recruitment coaching ($5k–$15k packages).
- **Different from Alma:** human coaches, no software. Targets the same wedge (kids who want IB) but priced for the top-1% spender. Alma's wedge is the Brown/Rice student who can't afford WSM but still wants the offer.

### Officehours.io
- Paid alumni mentor calls ($30–100/hr per session).
- **Different from Alma:** pay-per-call, not relationship-building. The Officehours mentor never becomes the user's referral.

### Wall Street Oasis ("WSO Networking Guide")
- $200 PDF + spreadsheet on how to network manually.
- **Different from Alma:** manual playbook, not software. Their thesis ("network ≫ resume") is exactly Alma's — we automate the doing.

### Mergers & Inquisitions
- Content + courses on the IB recruiting playbook.
- **Different from Alma:** education + community, no outreach automation.

## Indirect

### University career services + alumni directories (BrownConnect+, Rice Alumni)
- Free, school-run, low-engagement.
- **Different from Alma:** static directories, no drafting, no reply tracking, no agent. *We integrate them as a data source.*

### LinkedIn (organic)
- The default tool every student uses today.
- **Different from Alma:** the user has to do every step manually — search, find email, write the message, track who replied, remember to follow up. We compress all of that into one queue + send a real-banker-voiced email.

## Alma's differentiation in one sentence

> An AI agent that lives inside the entire IB recruiting funnel — finds the right banker for *you*, drafts the email in *your* voice, sends from *your* Gmail, tracks the reply, and advances you through coffee → referral → offer — for $0 if you're a Brown or Rice student.

## YC-style "why now" beat

1. **Banker-side**: outbound at scale is annoying for the banker, but a *good*, specific email from a Brown student will always get read. The bottleneck is the student writing 120 of those.
2. **Student-side**: the funnel math (120 calls → 1 offer) is unforgiving. Most students hit ~30 calls and burn out. The agent removes the activation energy.
3. **Tooling**: GPT-3 in 2022 couldn't write an email a banker would respond to. Sonnet 4 + a Critic loop + verbatim-only fact-checking *can*. We tested it.

## "Won't a generic outreach AI eat your lunch?"

No, for three reasons:
1. **Domain depth**: our firm + group + tier hierarchy + IB-specific funnel + warmth scoring would take a generalist a year to replicate. The wedge is narrow on purpose.
2. **Voice fingerprint**: every reviewer in IB recruiting can spot a "smart networking email generated by AI." Our Critic + fact-checker enforce verbatim-only and ban the AI tells. A horizontal sales tool can't afford to be that opinionated.
3. **Distribution**: students hear about Alma from other students at Brown/Rice. Network effect on a wedge.

## What we don't do (and why that's the right call right now)

- Not a job board. Not a course. Not a coaching service. Not a generic networking AI.
- Not multi-industry yet (consulting, PE, hedge funds, tech are post-launch wedges).
- Not Sales Navigator. Not Apollo. Not LinkedIn.

## Risks YC will probe

- **LinkedIn ToS** — we use Serper (Google search), not direct scraping. Fine.
- **Banker fatigue** — same banker getting 5 Alma emails would notice. Mitigation: per-banker dedup at DB level, response-rate flywheel, one-tester-per-firm-per-week soft-cap on the roadmap.
- **Hallucination** — fact-checker is the answer. False negatives caught in the Maya Patel audit; false positives caught in the 59-banker clean run.
- **Defensibility** — see "Alma's differentiation" above. Wedge depth + Critic calibration data + flywheel scoring weights = the moat.

## The two questions Alma gets in every conversation

### "Hasn't this been done already?"

No, not the full stack. There's:
- AI mock interview tools (Forage, super-day, breakin)
- AI sales sequencers (Lavender, Lemlist, Apollo)
- Manual playbooks (WSO, M&I)
- Coaching ($5–15k packages)

Nobody combines real banker discovery + per-banker fact-checking + student-voice drafting + Gmail send + reply tracking + 7-stage IB pipeline + anti-spam dedup, in one funnel for one demographic. **The wedge is the moat.**

### "If everyone uses this, no banker will respond."

Three answers, escalating:

1. **Volume cap.** Even with 1000 Alma users, no banker receives more than 1 email per Alma user per banker (per-banker dedup at the DB level). Today bankers already get 30+ cold emails/week from non-Alma students writing the same generic stuff. Alma's worst case = same volume. Best case = lower volume + higher quality.

2. **Anti-spam soft-cap (roadmap).** Across all Alma users, we cap how many can email the same banker in the same week. So we throttle our own demand if it's overwhelming any one inbox. The flywheel scoring weights make this profitable for us — bankers with high reply rates get prioritized for users with strongest fit, and over-saturated bankers get rotated out.

3. **Voice differentiation.** Even if 100 Alma students email Goldman, each writes from their own background to a banker who shares something specific with them. They don't all read like form letters — that's the fact-checker enforcing verbatim-only. Banker fatigue scales with quality, not volume.

Pitch it as: *AI doesn't get banker fatigue — bad outreach does. The bar is reply rate per email; ours is 22% in pilot vs ~3% manual. As we scale, we tune toward higher-quality fewer-emails, not the other way.*
